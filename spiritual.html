SPIRITUAL PROGRESSION PORTFOLIO

4.2 Expansion and Deepening

Jean-Marc Mukendi | Ensign College | IT 497 / Capstone | November 2026

Introduction

My spiritual progression and professional development are connected. As I continue my Bachelor of Science in Information Technology studies and build experience in database administration, data migration, and IT support, I want my choices to reflect my faith in Jesus Christ. Technical competence matters, but it must be guided by character, integrity, humility, and a sincere desire to serve. This expanded portfolio develops the three principles introduced in my earlier portfolio: Faith and Trust in God, Integrity and Service, and Continuous Learning and Growth.

Guiding Principle 1: Faith and Trust in God

Faith and trust in God help me remain grounded when I face uncertainty, pressure, or difficult decisions. I cannot control every result in my education or career, but I can prepare carefully, seek guidance through prayer and scripture, and act responsibly with the knowledge I have. Doctrine and Covenants 58:26–28 teaches the importance of agency and being anxiously engaged in a good cause. I understand this as an invitation to take initiative instead of waiting for every instruction or perfect condition.

Professional connection and reflection

In IT operations, unexpected incidents, migration issues, and deadlines can create stress. Faith does not replace technical analysis or responsibility. It helps me approach challenges with patience, courage, and perspective. During work involving core banking systems and data migration, I learned that I must prepare, verify information, communicate clearly, and ask for help when needed. Trusting God means doing my part diligently while accepting that some outcomes require patience and continued effort.

Action commitment: I will begin important decisions with thoughtful preparation and prayer, avoid reacting impulsively under pressure, and evaluate whether my choices reflect my values.

Guiding Principle 2: Integrity and Service

Integrity means being truthful, dependable, accountable, and respectful even when no one is watching. Service means using my knowledge and abilities to help others. Mosiah 2:17 connects service to serving God. In information technology, service is practical: systems must be reliable, information must be protected, and users deserve clear communication and respectful support.

Professional connection and reflection

My experience with database administration, banking channels, and application deployments has shown me that technical work affects real people. A careless change can interrupt a service; inaccurate data can create operational problems; and poor access control can expose private information. Integrity therefore requires honest reporting of test results, respect for authorization, careful documentation, and transparency when an error occurs. Service also means listening to users, explaining technical issues in understandable language, and helping colleagues become more capable.

Action commitment: I will protect confidential information, use only authorized access, document work accurately, report risks honestly, and take responsibility for correcting mistakes.

Guiding Principle 3: Continuous Learning and Growth

Continuous learning and growth recognize that technology and people both change. I want to keep developing in database administration, cloud computing, cybersecurity, and IT infrastructure while improving communication, collaboration, and leadership. Growth also requires humility: I must be willing to receive feedback, recognize gaps in my knowledge, and learn from mistakes rather than hide them.

Professional connection and reflection

Working with SQL Server, Oracle, core banking platforms, and digital delivery channels has taught me that no professional knows everything. New systems and business needs require research, testing, and collaboration. My studies at Ensign College also remind me that progress comes from consistent effort. Doctrine and Covenants 58:26–28 encourages initiative and purposeful action. I connect this teaching with setting learning goals, practicing skills, and applying what I learn to benefit others.

Action commitment: I will set measurable learning goals, seek feedback from instructors and colleagues, keep a record of lessons learned, and apply new knowledge responsibly.

Expanded spiritual materials and personal insights

3 Nephi 13:19–22 — Faith and Trust in God. This passage invites me to examine what I value most. It reminds me that career progress and material success should not replace spiritual priorities.

Proverbs 3:5–6 — Faith and Trust in God. I take this as encouragement to seek the Lord's direction while making thoughtful decisions about education, work, and the future.

Mosiah 2:17 — Integrity and Service. This scripture connects service to discipleship. In my work, helping users and protecting the systems they depend on can be an expression of service.

Matthew 5:14–16 — Integrity and Service. My conduct can influence others. Professional reliability, honesty, and respect can make a positive difference even when the work is not visible.

Doctrine and Covenants 58:26–28 — Continuous Learning and Growth. These verses encourage agency and initiative. I should not wait passively for growth; I should act, learn, and use my abilities for good.

David A. Bednar, “Gather Together in One All Things in Christ” (2018) — Integration of faith and life. This message helps me consider how faith, education, work, and relationships can support one another rather than remain separate parts of my life.

“Come What May, and Love It” (assigned course resource) — Faith and perseverance. The message encourages a faithful response to challenges. I can apply this by remaining hopeful and responsible when technical or academic work becomes difficult.

“Be Not Afraid, Only Believe” (assigned course resource) — Faith and courage. This resource reminds me not to let fear prevent responsible action. In professional settings, courage includes raising concerns and asking questions when something is unclear.

Personal reflection: connecting agency, faith, and work

When I look at my development, I see that progress has required decisions and consistent effort. I have had to choose to continue learning, solve unfamiliar problems, and accept responsibilities that affect other people. The idea of agency in Doctrine and Covenants 58:26–28 helps me understand that growth is not passive. I can choose how I respond to a challenge, whether I prepare carefully, whether I ask for feedback, and whether I act with integrity.

I also recognize that professional success should not be measured only by technical achievements or job titles. A reliable IT professional must be trustworthy. When I work with databases or systems that support financial services, I must remember that information represents people and that service availability can affect their daily lives. My faith encourages me to treat these responsibilities with care, humility, and respect.

Reflection helps me turn experience into learning. After a project or difficult task, I can ask: What went well? What did I not understand? Who was affected by my decisions? Did I communicate honestly? What should I do differently next time? These questions help me recognize both technical and personal areas for improvement. Going forward, I want my education and career to develop alongside my character. My goal is to become a capable IT professional who uses knowledge to serve others while remaining faithful to my spiritual priorities.
